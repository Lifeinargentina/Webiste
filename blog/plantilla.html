The 183-Day Rule: Are you a tax resident in Argentina?

By Martin Velasco

Head of Corporate

Many expats assume that if they don't have a DNI, they don't owe taxes. This is a common misconception that can lead to significant penalties. In Argentina, fiscal residency is determined primarily by the "center of vital interests" and the time spent in the country.

How the rule works

If you spend more than 183 days in a 12-month period within Argentine territory, AFIP (Administración Federal de Ingresos Públicos) may consider you a fiscal resident. This applies regardless of your visa status.

  • Worldwide Income: Residents are taxed on global income.
  • Personal Assets Tax: Applied to assets held worldwide.
  • Double Taxation Treaties: Check if your country has an agreement with Argentina to avoid paying twice.

Planning ahead

Before you hit the 6-month mark, it is crucial to speak with an accountant. Structuring your assets correctly before becoming a resident can save you thousands of dollars in the long run.
