# I’m already in Argentina as a tourist. Can I switch to residency without leaving?

**Short answer: sometimes.**
If you are already in Argentina as a tourist, you may be able to apply for residency without leaving the country. Whether you can do it depends first on your nationality and how you were allowed to enter Argentina.
For many people from the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Europe, Russia, Australia and other countries that normally enter Argentina without obtaining a tourist visa in advance, applying for residency from inside Argentina is possible.
For people who needed a consular visa, an Electronic Travel Authorization (AVE), or another form of prior authorization to enter, the answer can be different.
There is also an important third group that has become more relevant since 2025: people who can now enter Argentina without an Argentine visa because they hold a qualifying U.S. visa or Green Card. That does not necessarily mean they can switch to residency once they are here.
This distinction matters particularly for nationals of India, China and the Dominican Republic.

## First, check how Argentina admitted you

The easiest way to understand the rule is to start with your entry into Argentina.

| **Your situation**                                                                                   | **Can you generally apply for residency from Argentina?** |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| You are from a country that normally enters Argentina visa-free as a tourist                         | Usually yes, if you qualify for a residence category      |
| You needed an Argentine tourist visa before travelling                                               | Generally no                                              |
| You entered using an AVE or another prior authorization                                              | Generally no                                              |
| You qualify for family reunification                                                                 | Possibly yes, even if you normally needed a visa          |
| You are Indian, Chinese or Dominican and entered under the special U.S. visa or Green Card exemption | Generally no under the special 2025 rules                 |
| Your case has exceptional circumstances                                                              | Migraciones may authorize a change in limited cases       |

The legal basis for the general rule is DNM Disposition 1520/2024. It allows certain people to request a **cambio de categoría migratoria (change of immigration category)** from inside Argentina, including nationals of countries exempt from prior tourist visas and people applying on qualifying family reunification grounds.

That does not mean residency is automatic.
It only means that Argentina may allow you to make the application without first leaving the country.

## The distinction most people miss: visa-free entry does not always mean you can change status

This is where the current rules become more complicated.
In 2025, Argentina introduced special entry exemptions for certain nationals of **India, China and the Dominican Republic** who hold qualifying U.S. visas or, under the current rules, certain U.S. permanent residence documents.
These travelers may be able to enter Argentina for tourism without obtaining an Argentine consular visa or AVE.
But the resolutions creating these exemptions specifically state that their beneficiaries **cannot request a change of immigration category**.
So these two statements can both be true:
**You were allowed to enter Argentina without an Argentine tourist visa.**
**You are not allowed to convert that tourist entry into residency from inside Argentina.**
That is why simply asking, “Did I need a visa to come to Argentina?” is no longer enough.
You also need to know **why you were exempt from the visa requirement**.

## A common example: Indian citizens already in Argentina

We receive a lot of questions about this situation.
An Indian citizen may now be able to enter Argentina for tourism without an Argentine consular visa if they meet the requirements of the special exemption introduced in 2025, including holding one of the qualifying U.S. visas or other accepted U.S. immigration documents.
However, Resolution 353/2025 specifically provides that beneficiaries of this exemption cannot request a change of immigration category.
So an Indian citizen who arrives in Buenos Aires under this particular tourist exemption should not assume that they can simply open RaDEX and convert their status to student, rentista or another residence.
The way you entered matters.
The same problem can arise for Chinese and Dominican nationals using the special exemption established under Resolution 316/2025. That resolution also expressly prevents its beneficiaries from requesting a change of immigration category.
This is one of the reasons we recommend checking immigration strategy before preparing the rest of the residency file.

## What if I am American, British, Canadian, Russian or European?

These are much more straightforward cases in practice.
For example, U.S., British, Canadian and Russian passport holders appear on Argentina's ordinary list of nationalities that can enter for tourism without obtaining an Argentine visa in advance. The same applies to most European Union nationalities.
If you entered Argentina normally as a tourist and later decide that you want to stay, you may generally be able to request a change of immigration category from inside Argentina.
You still need a legal basis for the residence.
For example:

- employment;
- study;
- rentista income;
- pension;
- MERCOSUR nationality;
- family reunification; or
- another residence category recognized by Argentine immigration law.

Being American, British or European does not itself give you Argentine residency.
It can make the **in-country application route** available to you.

## What if I am from a MERCOSUR country?

For many South American nationals, the situation is simpler because nationality itself can provide the basis for temporary residence.
Nationals of qualifying MERCOSUR member and associated states can apply for residence on nationality grounds rather than having to demonstrate employment, studies or passive income.
This commonly includes nationals of countries such as Brazil, Uruguay, Paraguay, Bolivia, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador and Peru.
If you are already in Argentina after a regular entry, the residence application can generally be initiated through RaDEX.

## Being allowed to change category is only step one

A common misunderstanding is that a tourist can “convert” their 90-day stay into residency simply because they want to remain in Argentina.
There is no general residency for people who simply decide to stay longer.
You need to qualify under an actual immigration category.
For example, someone may be able to request a change of category from inside Argentina but still be unable to obtain a rentista residence because their income does not meet the current requirements.
Another person may want student residence but be enrolled in a program that does not qualify.
Someone else may have an Argentine boyfriend or girlfriend but not yet have the legal relationship required for the residence category they are considering.
There are therefore two separate questions:
**1. Are you allowed to change immigration category from inside Argentina?**
**2. Do you qualify for the residence you want?**
You need a yes to both.

## Does RaDEX determine whether you are eligible?

No.
RaDEX is the **Sistema de Radicación a Distancia (Remote Residency System)** used to begin residence applications with the Dirección Nacional de Migraciones.
It is the filing system.
It does not create eligibility.
Being able to register, upload documents or begin a process online does not necessarily mean that Migraciones will accept the legal basis of the application.
Before filing, it is worth checking your nationality, your entry record and the particular residence category you intend to request.

## Do I need to leave Argentina and come back?

If you entered under the ordinary visa-exempt regime and qualify for a residence category, often no.
Leaving Argentina simply to return as a tourist usually adds nothing to the residence application.
If your nationality or type of entry does not allow an in-country change of category, however, leaving may form part of the correct process. Depending on the residence sought, you may need to obtain an immigration authorization or complete a consular procedure from outside Argentina.
The important point is that crossing into Uruguay for a few days and returning does not transform a tourist into a resident.
You return as a tourist unless another immigration status has been granted.

## What if I entered with an AVE?

This deserves separate attention.
Disposition 1520/2024 defines **visado previo (prior visa)** broadly. It expressly includes consular visas, AVEs, TIE24H authorizations and other forms of prior entry authorization.
That means someone who entered Argentina using an AVE should not assume that they have the same ability to change category as someone whose passport allowed them to enter without any prior authorization.
If you entered using an AVE and now want residence, your case should be reviewed before beginning the residency process.

## What if my nationality normally requires a visa?

The general rule is more restrictive.
If you required an Argentine visa or another form of prior authorization to enter as a tourist, changing immigration category from inside Argentina is generally unavailable under Disposition 1520/2024.
There are exceptions.
The most important one is **family reunification**.
Disposition 1520/2024 allows people requesting residence on qualifying family reunification grounds to request a change of category even when they entered with a prior visa requirement.
Migraciones also retains authority to authorize a change outside the ordinary rules when objective circumstances justify an exceptional decision.
That should be treated as an exception, not as a standard residence strategy.

## What if my spouse or family member is Argentine?

Your nationality may become less important if you qualify for residence through family reunification.
Current Argentine immigration law recognizes temporary family reunification residence for certain close relatives of Argentine citizens and Argentine residents, including qualifying spouses, parents and children.
Because family reunification is expressly included as an exception in the change-of-category rules, someone who would normally have difficulty changing status after entering as a tourist may still be able to apply from Argentina on that basis.
The family relationship itself must still qualify and be properly documented.
If your partner is Argentine but you are not married, the analysis is slightly different. We cover that separately in our guide on **getting Argentine residency through an Argentine partner**.

## What if my tourist stay is about to expire?

Do not leave this until the last minute.
Migraciones' published guidance states that requests involving a change of transitory immigration category should be made before the authorized transitory stay expires.
If your tourist period has already expired, that creates a separate immigration issue that should be reviewed together with the proposed residence application.
An expired tourist stay should not be treated as simply another version of a valid tourist stay.

## What documents should I start preparing?

The exact documents depend on the residence category, but many applications require some combination of:

- valid passport;
- proof of regular entry into Argentina;
- Argentine criminal record certificate;
- foreign criminal record certificates, when applicable;
- proof of domicile in Argentina;
- civil status documents, if relying on a family relationship;
- evidence supporting your particular residence category; and
- apostilles or legalizations for documents issued abroad.

Foreign documents may also need to be translated into Spanish by an Argentine **traductor público (sworn translator)** and legalized through the relevant translators' association.
The most time-consuming documents are often the ones that need to come from abroad.
For that reason, it usually makes sense to determine your residence category before ordering certificates and apostilles.

## A simple way to check your situation

Before filing anything, answer these four questions:
**1. What passport do you hold?**
**2. Did you enter with only your passport, or did you need an Argentine visa, AVE, U.S. visa-based exemption or another authorization?**
**3. What residence category are you relying on?**
**4. Is your current immigration stay still valid?**
Those four answers usually tell us whether an application can be prepared from Argentina or whether a different route is needed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I apply for Argentine residency while I am in Argentina as a tourist?

Yes, in many cases. If your nationality falls within Argentina's ordinary visa-exempt regime and you qualify for a recognized residence category, you may generally be able to apply from inside Argentina.

### I am American and already in Buenos Aires. Do I need to return to the United States to apply?

Usually not solely for the purpose of beginning an Argentine residence application. U.S. nationals can enter Argentina for tourism without obtaining an Argentine tourist visa in advance, and an eligible applicant can generally request the appropriate change of immigration category from Argentina.

### I am British. Can I enter as a tourist and apply for residency?

British passport holders are currently visa-exempt for ordinary tourist entry. If you qualify for a residence category, an in-country application may generally be possible.

### I am Russian and already living in Argentina as a tourist. Can I apply?

Russian passport holders are currently included in Argentina's ordinary tourist visa-exempt list. The next question is which residence category you qualify for.

### I am Indian and entered Argentina without an Argentine visa because I have a U.S. visa. Can I apply for residency?

Do not assume that you can.
Resolution 353/2025 permits certain Indian nationals with qualifying U.S. immigration documents to enter Argentina without an Argentine visa, but the same resolution expressly states that beneficiaries cannot request a change of immigration category.
This is a good example of why “visa-free entry” and “permission to change immigration category” are not the same thing.

### I am Chinese and entered using my U.S. visa. Can I switch to residency?

The special exemption created by Resolution 316/2025 allows qualifying Chinese nationals to enter without an Argentine visa or AVE in certain circumstances, but beneficiaries of that regime cannot request a change of immigration category.

### What if I have an Argentine spouse?

Family reunification is an express exception to the ordinary restrictions on changing immigration category. Depending on your relationship and documentation, you may be able to apply from Argentina even if your nationality would otherwise require a prior visa.

### Can I just go to Uruguay and come back?

Leaving and returning does not give you residence. Unless you have obtained another immigration status, you are simply making another request to enter Argentina as a tourist.
It is better to determine whether you can apply for residence before relying on repeated tourist entries.

### Is being in Argentina enough to apply through RaDEX?

No. Physical presence is only part of the process. You also need to be legally eligible to request the residence and, where a change of category is necessary, eligible to make that change from inside Argentina.

## Before you start the residency application

Argentina is still a country where many people can arrive as tourists and later apply for residence without flying home first.
But the rule is no longer as simple as “enter visa-free and apply through RaDEX.”
Nationality matters. The legal basis for your visa exemption matters. The way you entered matters. And the residence category you want matters.
This is particularly important for applicants from countries such as India, China and the Dominican Republic, where recent entry exemptions can look very similar to ordinary visa-free travel but have different consequences for residency.
