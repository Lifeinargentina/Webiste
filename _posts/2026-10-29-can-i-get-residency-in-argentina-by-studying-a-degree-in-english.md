# Can I Get Residency in Argentina by Studying a Degree in English?

*An overview of English-taught bachelor's degrees, master's programs, and university courses in Argentina, and which options may help international students obtain legal residency.*

Moving to Argentina is an attractive possibility for many people who want to experience life in South America, pursue higher education, or establish themselves in Buenos Aires. But for prospective students from the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and other English-speaking countries, one question often comes up before anything else: can you study at an Argentine university without speaking Spanish?

The answer is yes, although the options are more limited than you might expect.

Argentina has a substantial university system, with internationally recognized institutions offering undergraduate degrees, postgraduate qualifications, and specialized academic programs. Most courses are taught in Spanish, but certain universities have developed programs specifically for international students, including degrees with English-language instruction and master's programs taught entirely in English.

For foreign nationals considering a longer stay, these opportunities raise another question: **can enrolling in one of these programs help you obtain legal residency in Argentina?**

At Life in Argentina, we regularly assist international students who want to combine their educational plans with a legal immigration strategy. Some are interested in completing a university degree, while others already hold a bachelor's degree and are looking for a master's program that would allow them to spend a year or more in Buenos Aires.

The distinction between these programs matters. A degree recognized within Argentina's official education system may provide grounds for temporary student residency, while enrolling in a short course or international exchange program may lead to a different immigration status.

Below, we examine some of the English-language educational opportunities available in Argentina, explain their potential immigration implications, and identify what you should verify before paying tuition or making relocation arrangements.

## 1. Can you really study in Argentina without speaking Spanish?

Yes, but finding a suitable program requires some research.

Spanish remains the principal language of instruction at Argentine universities. Even institutions with extensive international partnerships usually teach most undergraduate and postgraduate degrees in Spanish.

However, English-language courses are increasingly available in business administration, finance, international relations, economics, and other disciplines with a strong international orientation.

The most important distinction is between a university offering individual courses in English and a university offering a complete degree that can be completed without taking classes in Spanish.

These are not necessarily the same thing.

Some universities have bachelor's degrees that incorporate English-language coursework into an otherwise bilingual curriculum. Others provide English-taught subjects primarily for exchange students, while certain business schools offer entire postgraduate programs in English.

For immigration purposes, the language of instruction is not the decisive factor. What matters is the legal status of the educational program, the recognition of the institution, and the category under which the student is enrolled.

This means that someone who does not speak Spanish can potentially qualify for student residency through an English-language program, provided that the relevant immigration requirements are satisfied.

UADE offers several degree programs with an international focus, including Global Business Management and Global Financial Management.

**Bachelor's Degree in Global Business Management (GBM)**

This undergraduate degree combines business administration, international trade, management, and international business. According to UADE, the program includes technical subjects taught and assessed in English, participation by international professors, and opportunities to pursue double degrees through agreements with European universities.

The curriculum is particularly relevant for students interested in careers with multinational companies or international business organizations.

However, students should not assume that every subject is taught in English. UADE's own materials describe English-language coursework, rather than guaranteeing that the entire degree can be completed exclusively in English.

**Bachelor's Degree in Global Financial Management (GFM)**

UADE also offers a degree focused on international finance and corporate financial management.

According to the university's published program description, approximately 50% of the courses are taught in English. The remaining coursework should therefore be considered when evaluating whether the program is suitable for a student without Spanish proficiency.

The degree may be particularly interesting for students interested in financial markets, investment management, and international companies.

**Could these degrees support a residency application?**

Potentially, yes. Both are identified by UADE as undergraduate degree programs. A foreign student admitted as a regular student into an officially recognized degree may be able to apply for temporary student residency.

Before enrolling, we recommend confirming the program's official recognition, admission requirements, Spanish-language expectations, and the university's ability to issue the enrollment documentation required by the Dirección Nacional de Migraciones.

## 3. Can I get residency in Argentina by studying an MBA in English?

For applicants who already hold an undergraduate degree, a postgraduate program may be more appropriate than starting another bachelor's degree.

One of the clearest examples of a full English-language postgraduate program in Argentina is offered by IAE Business School.

<text>**Location:** Pilar, Buenos Aires Province, Argentina</text>

**Program:** Full-Time MBA

**Language:** English

**Format:** Full-time, in person

**Published duration:** 10 months

**Official website:** https://www.iae.edu.ar

IAE Business School, part of Universidad Austral, offers a full-time Master of Business Administration delivered entirely in English.

The program is intended for professionals looking to develop management and leadership skills, particularly within the Latin American business environment.

Its curriculum combines business strategy, organizational management, leadership, entrepreneurship, and applied business projects. The international orientation of the program is reflected in both its academic content and its use of English as the language of instruction.

For someone who has already completed a bachelor's degree and is considering spending approximately a year in Argentina, this is an especially relevant option.

Unlike programs that merely offer selected courses in English, IAE explicitly advertises its full-time MBA as entirely English-taught and delivered in person.

**Could the IAE MBA qualify for student residency?**

A recognized master's degree pursued through regular enrollment can provide grounds for temporary student residency under Argentine immigration law.

However, admission to the MBA and eligibility for residency remain separate questions. Applicants should verify the university's official documentation and the applicable immigration procedure before assuming that admission automatically results in a permit.

Another practical point is location. IAE's program is associated with Universidad Austral's campus in Pilar, outside central Buenos Aires. Students planning to live in neighborhoods such as Palermo or Recoleta should take the commute into account when choosing accommodation.

Universidad Torcuato Di Tella is another institution worth considering for international students interested in economics, business, politics, law, and related fields.

This is particularly interesting for students who want to spend part of their university education in Argentina without needing to complete every course in Spanish.

The institution accepts international students through university partnerships and independent study arrangements, subject to the relevant admission requirements.

For courses taught in English, Spanish proficiency is not required. Students whose native or previous academic language is not English may need to provide evidence of English proficiency at B2 level.

**Can these courses be used to obtain residency?**

This depends on the type of enrollment.

A student enrolling for one semester through an international mobility program is not necessarily treated in the same way as someone admitted to a complete, officially recognized degree.

Under Argentina's student immigration regulations, certain international mobility arrangements qualify for transitory residency rather than temporary residency.

That distinction affects the student's immigration status and the possibility of obtaining an Argentine DNI.

UTDT is therefore worth exploring for international academic mobility, but applicants seeking longer-term temporary residency should establish which specific degree or enrollment arrangement would support their immigration application.

Argentina's immigration regulations distinguish between students enrolled in recognized degree programs and individuals participating in international mobility, non-degree education, or other learning activities.

Many of these shorter programs may support transitory student status, but they should not be advertised as guaranteed pathways to temporary residency or an Argentine DNI.

Before enrolling in a course primarily for immigration purposes, we recommend obtaining written confirmation of the program's legal classification and the documentation the university can issue.

## 8. What does Argentine immigration law say about student residency?

Argentina recognizes student immigration status under Article 23(j) of Immigration Law No. 25,871 and the regulations implemented by the Dirección Nacional de Migraciones.

The legal framework includes individuals enrolled as regular students in officially recognized secondary, tertiary, university, and postgraduate programs.

Disposition No. 2802/2023 further distinguishes several categories of international students.

For someone planning to pursue a complete, officially recognized university degree, Article 7 of the regulation is particularly relevant.

It provides for temporary residency for eligible students enrolled in the official education system, for a period of up to two years under that regulatory framework, subject to renewal and applicable administrative requirements.

For other academic activities, such as certain exchange programs and non-degree courses, the regulations establish different immigration categories that may provide transitory residency for up to one year.

This distinction is essential because Argentine temporary and transitory residency do not provide identical legal rights.

A temporary resident may qualify for an Argentine DNI, while transitory student status generally does not lead to issuance of a DNI.

For this reason, the first question we ask is not simply which university a client wants to attend, but precisely which program they intend to pursue and how the institution will register their enrollment.

## 9. Can I apply for student residency after entering Argentina as a tourist?

For eligible applicants who are lawfully present in Argentina, it may be possible to initiate a change of immigration status through the Dirección Nacional de Migraciones.

However, entering the country as a tourist does not guarantee that a person will subsequently qualify for student residency.

The applicant must satisfy the applicable immigration requirements, including valid identification, lawful entry, and proof of enrollment in an eligible educational program.

The institution must also be able to provide the documentation required under the relevant student category.

Applications made within Argentina are generally initiated through RaDEX, the electronic system used by the Dirección Nacional de Migraciones.

Applicants may also need to attend an appointment and provide original documents for verification.

We frequently recommend planning these steps before traveling, particularly when the university's academic calendar and the student's permitted tourist stay do not align.

## 10. What documents do I need to obtain student residency in Argentina?

The exact requirements depend on the applicant's nationality, academic program, and immigration category.

For a temporary student residency application, the documentation generally includes:

1. A valid passport or other accepted identity document.
2. Evidence of lawful entry into Argentina, when applying within the country.
3. Criminal background certificates, where required, from the relevant countries of prior residence.
4. Proof of address in Argentina.
5. The electronic enrollment certificate issued through the applicable immigration procedure by the educational institution.
6. Any additional documentation requested by the Dirección Nacional de Migraciones.

Documents issued abroad may need to be apostilled or otherwise legalized and translated into Spanish by an authorized translator.

A university's ordinary admission letter is not necessarily a substitute for the specific electronic enrollment documentation required by Migraciones.

## 11. Can I get student residency through an online degree?

This is an important distinction for people who already work remotely or intend to remain professionally active while studying.

Enrolling in a degree offered online does not automatically establish eligibility for Argentine student residency.

The existence of an academically recognized program is only one part of the analysis. Its teaching modality, the student's actual academic activities in Argentina, and the purpose of the immigration category must also be considered.

For example, enrolling in an entirely online master's degree offered by an overseas university would not, by itself, establish the same immigration basis as attending an officially recognized Argentine university program in person.

Even where an Argentine institution offers an online degree, applicants should not assume that remote enrollment is sufficient for temporary student residency.

The institution and the Dirección Nacional de Migraciones should be consulted regarding the specific program before using it as the basis of an immigration application.

This is particularly important for applicants whose main objective is relocating to Argentina rather than obtaining a qualification remotely.

## 12. Can my student residency eventually lead to permanent residency?

Student residency can provide a lawful basis for living in Argentina while completing an eligible academic program.

However, it is important to distinguish between obtaining temporary student residency, renewing that residency, changing immigration categories, and qualifying for permanent residency.

These are different procedures with different requirements.

A student should not assume that completing a university degree automatically entitles them to permanent residency or Argentine citizenship.

The options available after graduation depend on the immigration framework in force, the individual's nationality, residence history, actual time spent in Argentina, and whether they qualify under another immigration category.

For clients who intend to remain in Argentina after graduation, we prefer to examine these possibilities before the initial enrollment.

That approach helps ensure that academic and immigration decisions are considered together, rather than treating residency as an issue that only arises when the course ends.
