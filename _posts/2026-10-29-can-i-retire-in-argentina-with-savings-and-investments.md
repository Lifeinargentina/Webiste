# Can I retire in Argentina with savings and investments?

*Last reviewed: October 29, 2026*

Potentially, yes. Savings, investment income or a foreign pension may support a residency application in Argentina, but having enough money to stop working does not automatically establish eligibility. The question is what your funds represent, how you can document them and which immigration category fits.

Argentina’s [Migration Law, article 23(b) and (c)](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-25871-92016/actualizacion), provides two relevant routes: rentista and pensionado. There is also an important nuance: the rentista rules expressly contemplate certain funds held in foreign bank accounts. Savings should therefore neither be dismissed outright nor treated as a guaranteed route.

<a id="savings-alone"></a>

## Can I retire in Argentina with savings alone?

There is a legal basis to examine this possibility. Article 3 of [DNM Disposition 1732/2023, as amended](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/disposici%C3%B3n-1732-2023-384788/actualizacion), admits an applicant’s own resources deposited in foreign bank accounts when their lawful origin is conclusively established.

However, the [published in-country rentista checklist](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/obtener-una-residencia-temporaria-como-rentista) focuses on income from assets. It does not explain a complete assessment method for someone living entirely from accumulated savings.

This distinction matters. A bank statement shows what you have today. It may not explain where the money came from, whether it is fully available to you or how it will support your stay. A savings-based application needs individual review, including the evidence and transfer arrangements Migraciones will require.

<a id="investment-income"></a>

## Can investment income qualify me for residency?

It may support a rentista application. The [official guidance](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/obtener-una-residencia-temporaria-como-rentista) covers benefits from financial instruments, real estate and interests in companies. It excludes remuneration for personal work.

Interest, dividends and rental payments are therefore sources to examine within those asset categories, rather than automatic approvals. For example, owning a portfolio and receiving documented interest from it are different facts. Similarly, owning shares does not establish how much dividend income you actually receive.

For an initial review, it helps to connect three things: the asset you own, the payment it produces and the account receiving it. Irregular dividends, reinvested earnings or payments mixing profits with returned capital need closer examination. The published guidance does not provide a universal formula for averaging those payments into monthly qualifying income.

<a id="retirement-accounts"></a>

## Can I use my IRA or 401(k) to get residency?

Possibly, but the account name is not enough. The Argentine official sources reviewed for this article do not establish a blanket rule accepting or rejecting IRA or 401(k) distributions.

These are examples of foreign retirement arrangements, not Argentine immigration categories. Even within a 401(k), payments can take different forms, including lump sums, installments or annuities, depending on the plan. The [IRS distribution guidance](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/401k-resource-guide-plan-participants-general-distribution-rules) illustrates that variety; it does not determine Argentine residency eligibility.

Before choosing a route, ask the custodian or plan administrator what it can certify: account ownership, the type of arrangement, access restrictions, payment amounts and frequency, and the duration or terms of the entitlement. Where possible, obtain records explaining whether distributions represent investment returns, a drawdown of principal or a retirement benefit.

A person choosing to withdraw money from an investment account is not necessarily in the same position as someone entitled to a regular pension. For pensionado, the [DNM checklist](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/obtener-una-residencia-temporaria-como-pensionado) requires certification of a regular and permanent pension or retirement benefit from the relevant government, international organization or private company. Whether a particular arrangement meets that description needs assessment.

<a id="regular-withdrawals"></a>

## Do regular withdrawals from savings count as income?

A monthly transfer does not, by itself, establish the nature of the money. Selling investments or withdrawing cash can reduce your existing capital rather than generate a new return.

Imagine two people receiving identical monthly transfers. One receives interest paid on a deposit. The other transfers part of an existing balance to a spending account. Their cash flow looks similar, but their supporting evidence tells different stories.

The foreign-bank-funds provision discussed above may be relevant to the second person. That is different from relabelling every withdrawal as investment income. The official sources reviewed do not say that setting up an automatic withdrawal makes the underlying funds qualify, or explain how all portfolio sales should be assessed.

<a id="selling-home"></a>

## Can I qualify with money from selling my home?

A home sale can help explain the origin of your savings. It does not create a pension or a recurring rental payment.

If you retain a property and rent it out, the review concerns ownership, the lease and actual rent received. If you sell it, the review concerns the sale proceeds, where they are held and how they will support you. If you later invest them, any resulting income must be examined separately.

Useful records for that review may include proof of ownership, the completed sale agreement or closing statement, and bank records tracing receipt of the proceeds. These are suggested evidence, not a published guarantee of acceptance. A planned sale also differs from funds already received and documented.

<a id="rentista-pensionado"></a>

## What is the difference between rentista and pensionado residency?

**Rentista** concerns support through qualifying resources from abroad, with the administrative rules addressing asset income and certain foreign bank funds. **Pensionado** concerns a regular and permanent pension for services performed abroad, paid by a government, international organization or private company. These are the distinctions in [article 23 of the Migration Law](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-25871-92016/actualizacion).

Being retired in everyday life does not decide which route applies. Someone who stops working before becoming entitled to a pension may need a different assessment from someone already receiving one.

The current in-country DNM pages describe [rentista](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/obtener-una-residencia-temporaria-como-rentista) and [pensionado](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/obtener-una-residencia-temporaria-como-pensionado) residence as valid for one year, renewable. They should not be presented as immediate permanent residence.

**The application route matters.** DNM’s separate [pre-entry permit guidance](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/obtener-un-permiso-de-ingreso-temporario-como-rentista-o-pensionado) describes stable monthly rentista income for at least two years and specifies its own evidence. This is a published instruction for that procedure, not a two-year condition expressly stated in article 3 of Disposition 1732/2023. Confirm the applicable process before arranging documents or travel.

<a id="monthly-income"></a>

## How much monthly income do I need?

The benchmark for both categories is **five Argentine minimum monthly wages**, known as the salario mínimo, vital y móvil or SMVM. [Disposition 3446/2023](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/disposici%C3%B3n-3446-2023-392424/texto) establishes the five-SMVM threshold; DNM’s [permit guidance](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/obtener-un-permiso-de-ingreso-temporario-como-rentista-o-pensionado) expressly describes the income on a monthly basis.

As of October 29, 2026, the monthly SMVM is ARS 391,200. Five times that amount is **ARS 1,956,000**. This calculation uses the monthly wage, not the hourly rate, under [Resolution 4/2026, effective October 1](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/norma-429565/texto). That resolution schedules further increases, so this is a dated reference, not a permanent requirement.

There is no fixed US-dollar equivalent. Before applying, confirm the applicable SMVM and how Migraciones will assess foreign-currency payments. For a capital-based proposal, do not assume that multiplying this figure by a chosen number of months establishes eligibility.

<a id="spouse"></a>

## Can my spouse apply with me?

A spouse may have a family-reunification route linked to the principal applicant’s residence, rather than having to qualify independently as rentista or pensionado. DNM’s [current family-reunification guidance](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/obtener-una-residencia-temporaria-por-reunificacion-familiar) includes spouses of temporary residents and ties their residence period to that of the sponsoring resident.

This still requires a separate application, proof of the marriage and the sponsor’s identity and status. Plan the timing of both applications together; marriage does not automatically extend one person’s approval to the other.

Do not rely on older statements that five SMVM must always be shown for each family member. [Disposition 3446/2023](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/disposici%C3%B3n-3446-2023-392424/texto) replaced the provisions containing that wording. However, [Decree 616/2010, article 23(b)](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/decreto-616-2010-167004/texto), still requires sufficient rentista resources to support the applicant and their primary family group. Review household needs and account ownership rather than assuming either an automatic doubling or unrestricted pooling.

<a id="documents"></a>

## What documents should I prepare before moving?

Start with the requirements for your intended procedure. The [in-country DNM checklist](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/obtener-una-residencia-temporaria-como-pensionado) includes identity documents, lawful entry, an Argentine address and criminal-record documentation. For applicants over 16, it specifies foreign certificates from countries where they lived for more than one year during the preceding three years; the Argentine check is incorporated into RaDEX.

For pensionado, obtain the issuer’s benefit certificate. The separate [pre-entry procedure](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/obtener-un-permiso-de-ingreso-temporario-como-rentista-o-pensionado) additionally lists the pension award and the last three payment receipts; for rentista it requests certification from the entity remitting the funds.

For an individual financial review, we also suggest gathering:

- **Ownership and source records:** account statements, investment holdings and documents explaining how the capital was acquired.

- **Payment evidence:** interest or dividend statements, leases and rent receipts, or retirement-plan terms and distribution histories.

- **Family records:** marriage documentation and a clear account of who owns, receives and can access the funds.

These suggested records help assess the case; they are not a universal statutory checklist. Article 4 of [Disposition 1732/2023](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/disposici%C3%B3n-1732-2023-384788/actualizacion) requires verification, before approval, of funds entering Argentina through BCRA-authorized banking or financial institutions. Check the required amount and transfer evidence before moving money.

Confirm apostille or legalization and Spanish-translation formalities before ordering documents. DNM publishes those formalities in its [rentista renewal instructions](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/node/35429); for a consular application, obtain the checklist from the [responsible Argentine consulate](https://www.cancilleria.gob.ar/es/servicios/servicios-para-extranjeros/visas).

<a id="before-moving"></a>

## Before you organize the move

Immigration residence and tax residence answer different questions. One concerns permission to live in Argentina; the other concerns tax obligations. Under [the Income Tax Law, article 116](https://biblioteca.afip.gob.ar/dcp/LEY_C_020628_2019_12_05), foreign nationals can become tax residents through permanent residence or a qualifying period of temporary residence. Obtain a separate tax review of foreign investments, pensions and any planned property sale. Immigration approval does not promise favorable tax treatment.

Before choosing a moving date, establish what you own, what you receive, how long those payments can continue and which documents demonstrate it. Include your spouse’s situation and the ownership of any joint accounts. Those details are more useful than a portfolio total alone.
