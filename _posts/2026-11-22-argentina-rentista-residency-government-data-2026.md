## How many Rentista residencies did Argentina grant in 2024–2026?

According to the official DNM records supplied to Life in Argentina, **869 temporary residencies were granted under the RENTISTA subcategory from January 2024 through September 2026**.

| Period | Rentista residencies granted |
|:--|--:|
| 2024 (full year) | 425 |
| 2025 (full year) | 238 |
| January–September 2026 | 206 |
| **Total** | **869** |

*Source: Dirección Nacional de Migraciones, official residency-grant dataset provided to Life in Argentina in response to a formal public-information request. Category: temporary residency; subcategory: RENTISTA.*

This is a relatively specialized immigration pathway. In 2025, the DNM recorded **238 Rentista residency grants out of 71,457 temporary residency grants** across all subcategories—about **0.33%**.

The annual number fell from 425 in 2024 to 238 in 2025. During just the first nine months of 2026, another 206 grants had already been recorded. Since the 2026 series ends in September, it should **not** be compared with a complete calendar year as though the periods were equivalent.

These figures establish that Rentista is an actively used legal residency category. They **do not** tell us how many applications were submitted, how many were denied, or an individual applicant's probability of approval. Nor do the category-level numbers identify whether beneficiaries relied on rental property, financial investments, or other qualifying income.

That distinction matters: **a recorded residency grant is not the same thing as a new arrival, a successful application rate, or proof that one source of income is officially favored**.

## Why Rentista residency can be one of the most attractive options

One common assumption about moving abroad is that you must first find a job in your destination country. Argentina offers another possibility for qualifying applicants.

Under [Article 23(b) of Immigration Law No. 25,871](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/ley-25871-92016), the Rentista category provides for temporary residency for people supporting themselves through qualifying income connected to resources abroad. The [DNM's current procedural guidance](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/obtener-una-residencia-temporaria-como-rentista) describes qualifying income from assets in the applicant's estate, including real estate, financial instruments, and company interests, and excludes compensation for personal work from that definition.

Consider someone who owns an apartment in New York, Miami, London, or Madrid and receives rental payments every month. Rather than moving to Argentina to search for local employment, that person may be able to document the property and its income as the basis of a Rentista application.

For the right applicant, this route is attractive because it may allow them to:

- Maintain income-generating assets abroad while settling in Argentina.
- Apply for residency without depending on an Argentine employer.
- Plan a longer-term move around an existing, documented source of income.
- Explore a lawful residency strategy for a family, subject to the requirements applicable to each applicant.

**Rentista is not an automatic or guaranteed residency.** The income must meet the governing criteria, including its source, amount, continuity, documentation, and the requirements concerning funds entering Argentina.

## Why we pay particular attention to income from rental properties

In our immigration practice, **rental income from property abroad is one of the clearest forms of income to prepare and explain when the underlying facts and documents are strong**.

A genuine rental arrangement often allows us to build a traceable documentary record: the client owns an asset, the asset produces income, and payments arrive on a recurring basis. Depending on the case, relevant documentation may include:

- The property's title or other acceptable evidence of ownership.
- A valid lease or rental agreement.
- Bank statements showing actual rental payments.
- Tax or accounting records supporting the income's lawful origin and consistency.
- The foreign documentation, authentication, and translations required for the Argentine application.

**Based on our practical experience**, a coherent rental-income file can be easier for an immigration officer to evaluate because the relationship between the asset and the income is clear and verifiable.

This is **our professional observation**, not a published policy or formal preference of the Dirección Nacional de Migraciones. Owning rental property does not confer priority, waive documentary requirements, or guarantee an approval. The authorities assess each application on its merits.

For us, the real advantage is the possibility of constructing a transparent and well-supported case before the application is filed.

## What if your income does not come from renting a property?

**You do not necessarily need to be a landlord to explore Rentista residency.** Argentina's official guidance also refers to benefits generated by other assets, including financial instruments and ownership interests in companies.

Depending on the arrangement, relevant income might arise from investment holdings, dividends, or other returns generated by assets owned by the applicant. However, not every payment received from overseas qualifies.

There are important distinctions:

- **Passive income from owned assets:** potentially relevant to Rentista, provided the facts and documentation satisfy the law.
- **Salary or freelance income earned through personal services:** not treated as Rentista asset income under the DNM's published guidance.
- **Savings or a large bank balance:** not automatically equivalent to qualifying recurring income.
- **A complicated mix of distributions, investments, and business income:** requires individual analysis of the underlying asset and the nature of each payment.

This is where a specialized immigration practice becomes useful. At Life in Argentina, we evaluate **what produces the money, who owns the asset, how regularly the income is received, and what evidence can prove each point**.

Sometimes Rentista is the appropriate route. In other cases, a pensioner, employment-based, family-based, or another residency category may fit the person's circumstances better. Our role is to identify the defensible strategy, not to place every client into the same category.

## What are the Rentista residency requirements in Argentina in 2026?

The DNM's [Rentista residency page](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/obtener-una-residencia-temporaria-como-rentista) identifies several requirements. They include:

1. **Qualifying income:** documented income equal to or above **five Argentine minimum monthly wages (SMVM)**. Since the minimum wage changes, the amount in pesos must be checked when preparing the application.
2. **Proof of the underlying asset and income:** evidence that income results from qualifying assets belonging to the applicant, rather than remuneration for personal work.
3. **Transfer of funds:** evidence that the required funds enter Argentina through banking or financial channels authorized by the Central Bank of Argentina, as required by the applicable rules.
4. **Personal and immigration documents:** a valid passport or permitted travel document, applicable criminal-record certificates, proof of address, lawful entry, and other documents requested in the procedure.
5. **Formalities for foreign documents:** legalization or apostille and Spanish translations where required.

A key point for families: [DNM Disposition 1732/2023](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/disposici%C3%B3n-1732-2023-384788) provides that the required income threshold must be demonstrated **for each residency applicant in family groups**. Eligibility and documentation therefore need to be considered carefully for every family member, rather than assuming that one person's qualifying income automatically covers everyone.

The [official Rentista procedure](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/servicio/obtener-una-residencia-temporaria-como-rentista) currently describes an initial **one-year temporary residence, renewable**. The statutory category itself permits temporary residence for up to three years; the period actually granted depends on the applicable procedure and administrative decision.

Most importantly, meeting an income figure by itself is not enough. The **legal character and documentary traceability of the income** are essential.
